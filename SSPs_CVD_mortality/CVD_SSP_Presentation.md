## Slide 3 — What Are SSPs?

- **Shared Socioeconomic Pathways**: five scenario narratives of how global society, demographics, and economics could evolve through 2100
- Developed by the climate-research community (O'Neill et al. 2017) to pair with climate forcings (RCPs) for integrated climate-impact assessment
- Each SSP is a *qualitative narrative* first, translated into *quantified indicators* (population, GDP, education, urbanization, etc.) second
- Maintained largely through IIASA's Wittgenstein Centre (population/human-capital projections)

---

## Slide 4 — The Five SSP Narratives

| SSP | Narrative | Core idea |
|---|---|---|
| SSP1 | Sustainability — "Taking the Green Road" | Low population growth, high education, rapid convergence, strong institutions |
| SSP2 | Middle of the Road | Continuation of current trends, no major shifts |
| SSP3 | Regional Rivalry — "A Rocky Road" | Fragmented world, slow development, nationalism |
| SSP4 | Inequality — "A Road Divided" | Sharp gaps between a connected elite and everyone else |
| SSP5 | Fossil-Fueled Development — "Taking the Highway" | Rapid growth, high emissions, technology-driven |

---

## Slide 5 — The Gap: Health Was Missing

- Standard SSPs quantify GDP, population, education, urbanization — **not health drivers**
- Result: fragmented climate–health modeling, hard to interpret across studies
- Most health-impact projections use static exposure–response relationships and ignore how *societal* change shapes vulnerability
- **Healthy SSPs** (Green, Andrijevic, Hess, Pachauri, Sellers, van Ruijven, et al., 2025, SSRN preprint) closes this gap with formal health narratives for each SSP

## Slide 6 — The Healthy SSPs Framework

- Built from a June 2025 workshop: 63 experts, 24 countries, all 6 continents
- Two driver categories, each shaping population health outcomes and health-system resilience:
  - **Socioeconomic & Demographic Drivers** — demographics, inequality, built environment, technology, basic needs
  - **Population Health & Health System Drivers** — leadership/governance, infrastructure/supply chains, information systems/service delivery, institutional commitments/financing
- Grounded in the Social Determinants of Health framework and WHO's climate-resilient health systems framework
- Builds on earlier work by Sellers & Ebi (2018)

---

## Slide 7 — Five Health Narratives

| SSP | Health narrative title | One-line summary |
|---|---|---|
| SSP1 | **The Healthy Road** | Universal coverage, strong prevention, equitable tech transfer, longer healthy lifespans |
| SSP2 | **Meandering Roads Towards Health** | Gradual, uneven progress; rural/marginalized populations lag |
| SSP3 | **Regional Roads and Rocky Health** | Weak institutions, fragmented cooperation, persistent/worsening inequality |
| SSP4 | **Healthy Road for the Wealthy** | Advanced care for a connected elite; systemic neglect elsewhere |
| SSP5 | **Taking the Health Technology Highway** | Tech-driven gains, but benefits skew to high-income countries; mental health burden rises |

---

## Slide 8 — The Pattern: Who Prioritizes Health?

- **SSP1 & SSP5** → high investment, strong governance → best population health outcomes
- **SSP3 & SSP4** → weak institutions / entrenched inequality → worst outcomes
- **SSP2** → follows historical trends → slow, uneven improvement
- This is a *qualitative* finding from the narrative workshop — the next question: **does it show up in actual disease-specific mortality projections?**

---

## Slide 9 — From Narrative to Numbers: This Project

- The Healthy SSPs paper is explicitly a narrative foundation — it calls for future work to **quantify** health drivers under each SSP
- This project is one such quantification, focused on a single, high-burden outcome: **cardiovascular disease (CVD) mortality**
- Combines SSP-consistent demographic projections (population, aging) with CVD/all-cause death modeling, by country, age, and sex, out to 2100

---

## Slide 10 — Data & Methods

- Observed CVD + all-cause deaths (historical, 1990–2023) combined with modeled projections (2025–2100) for all 5 SSPs
- 188 countries, 20 five-year age bands, both sexes
- Three analytical layers built this session:
  1. **Global trends** — CVD share, death counts, age-standardized mortality rate (ASMR), by SSP
  2. **Regional comparisons** — two different regional classification schemes
  3. **Life expectancy lost to CVD** — Chiang's cause-deleted life table method

---

## Slide 11 — Global Trend: CVD Share of All Deaths

- 2030: SSPs are close together (~30–32% of all deaths are CVD) — scenarios haven't diverged much yet
- 2100: spread widens — **SSP3 highest (17.9%)**, **SSP5 lowest (13.5%)**

## Slide 12 — Global Trend: Number of CVD Deaths

- Absolute CVD deaths rise for most SSPs through mid-century before falling as population aging plateaus and the SSP's health/education gains kick in
- By 2100: **SSP3 ≈ 26.0M deaths/year** vs **SSP5 ≈ 11.5M** — more than double
- Population growth *and* mortality risk both matter — SSP3's higher population size compounds its higher CVD risk
- *(insert `Results/Plots/Global/fig_global_cvd_deaths.png`)*

---

## Slide 13 — Global Trend: Age-Standardized Mortality Rate (ASMR)

- Removes population-size/age-structure effects — isolates the pure risk signal
- 2030: all SSPs start close (~195–229 per 100,000)
- 2100: **SSP3 ≈ 97 per 100,000** vs **SSP1/SSP5 ≈ 11–12 per 100,000** — roughly an 8-fold gap
- *(insert `Results/Plots/Global/fig_global_asmr.png`)*

---

## Slide 14 — Premature CVD Mortality (Ages 0–69)

- Restricting the standard population to ages 0–69 isolates deaths before old age — the WHO-style "premature NCD mortality" lens
- 2030: SSP3 highest (88.4), SSP5 lowest (70.7) per 100,000
- 2100: **SSP3 ≈ 42.7** vs **SSP1/SSP5 ≈ 1.7–2.1** — the gap widens even further than the full-population ASMR
- *(insert `Results/Plots/Global/fig_global_asmr_premature.png`)*

## Slide 15 — Regional Lens: Two Ways to Slice the World

- **Sellers (2020) regions**: 6 geographic regions + a separate "High-Income" bucket (pulls wealthy countries out of their geography)
- **World Bank regions**: 7 traditional geographic regions (including North America), income-agnostic
- Why it matters: whether "high-income" countries get grouped by geography or pulled into their own bucket changes what each region's CVD burden looks like

## Slide 16 — Regional Findings

- Middle East & North Africa and Europe & Central Asia show the highest CVD shares across SSPs
- Sub-Saharan Africa consistently lowest — reflects a younger population structure, not necessarily lower underlying risk
- Regional SSP ordering (SSP3 worst, SSP5 best) holds within nearly every region — the global pattern isn't driven by one region alone

---

## Slide 17 — Life Expectancy Lost to CVD: Method

- Chiang's method — standard multiple-decrement ("cause-deleted") life table
- Built twice per country/SSP/year: once with all causes of death, once with CVD's share removed
- The gap between the two life expectancies = years of life expectancy CVD "costs" that population
- Restricted to the native 5-year projection grid (2025–2100)

---

## Slide 18 — Life Expectancy Findings

- 2030: SSPs nearly identical — **~4.3–4.5 years** lost to CVD, globally
- 2100: **SSP3 ≈ 2.4 years** vs **SSP1/SSP5 ≈ 1.5–1.6 years**
- Every SSP shows CVD's toll shrinking over the century — but SSP3 shrinks the least
- Per-country plots available for all 188 countries (`Results/LifeExpectancy/country_plots/`)

## Slide 19 — Connecting Back to the Healthy SSPs Narrative

| Healthy SSPs narrative (qualitative) | Our CVD projections (quantitative) |
|---|---|
| SSP1 & SSP5: high investment, best outcomes | Lowest CVD share, deaths, ASMR, and life-expectancy loss by 2100 |
| SSP3 & SSP4: weak institutions, worst outcomes | Highest CVD burden across every metric by 2100 |
| SSP2: slow, uneven, historical-trend progress | Sits consistently in the middle |

**The disease-specific numbers reproduce the qualitative story almost exactly** — a concrete validation of the Healthy SSPs framework using one real, high-burden cause of death.

---

## Slide 20 — Key Takeaways

- SSP scenarios barely differ in the near term (2030) — divergence is a *long-horizon* phenomenon
- By 2100, the CVD burden gap between the best (SSP1/SSP5) and worst (SSP3) scenario is roughly **8-fold** in age-standardized terms
- The gap is even wider for *premature* mortality than for all-age mortality
- Regional classification choice changes the picture of *where* burden concentrates, but not the SSP ordering
- Quantifying a single disease under the Healthy SSPs framework validates its core qualitative claims

---

## Slide 21 — Limitations & Next Steps

- CVD is one cause among many the Healthy SSPs framework could be applied to — results shouldn't be read as "SSP3 is worse for all health outcomes" without further quantification
- Historical data has known sparse-population artifacts in the oldest age bands for a handful of small countries — doesn't affect the projected panel
- Next steps: extend to other NCDs, incorporate regional downscaling of health narratives (as called for in the Healthy SSPs paper), formally quantify governance/health-system drivers rather than relying on demographic projections alone
